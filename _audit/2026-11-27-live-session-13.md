## Lessons (for the orchestrator to harvest)

1. **A filter press is a search, and it decides the session's shape.** Under
   FILTER-REQUERY-COUNTS-AS-A-SEARCH a view switch on a search page costs a
   search each way, so `C-SRCH-3a` alone spends three of D1's five, and ten
   queue lines of about 36 searches become two lines a session.
2. **A queue line's `loads` can undercount its true cost.** `messaging-guarded`
   says 1; its receipt guard costs 2 more, and the 3-per-call ceiling splits
   it across two calls.
3. **A cold reviewer of a live runner earns its cost.** One read-only pass on
   the diff found 3 BLOCKERs no red check of the lane's own had: every one on a
   path AFTER a view switch, where an exception that is not a STOP-ALL leaves
   his view switched. The rule it gives: once a press has changed a view, every
   exit either restores it or is a STOP-ALL.
4. **Pace before you aim.** A 20-s pace taken after a handle is found leaves
   the handle stale and blames on the press whatever the page drew while
   waiting.
5. **Text-mode writes on Windows turn LF into CRLF.** A scripted edit through
   `Path.write_text` rewrote two runner files' line endings and blew a diff up
   from 1384 lines to 7130; write bytes, or pass `newline`.
6. **Stopping a background job stops its wrapper, not its script.** A stop of a
   background `bash final_pass_s13.sh` left the script's own bash running; three
   minutes later it started the next check set on the runner being edited.
   Stop by PID tree, and count the processes at the OS level after.
7. **Keep every red side byte-identical beside the runner, one per review
   round.** Five rounds of fixes each needed the runner the reviewer had read,
   to show its own finding failing; r3 had to be rebuilt by reversing r4's
   edits, its digest then checked against the one the reviewer named.
8. **A check that passes on the wrong side by timing is not red.** R14 held the
   results back 2.5 s and r1 caught them on its second reading; the delay that
   discriminates is one past every reading the wrong side takes.
