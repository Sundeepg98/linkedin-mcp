## 2. THE DEEPEST FINDING: THE FILTER, NOT THE MATCHER

The round was assigned as *"the 233 cross-slice disagreements"*. Every precision
figure anyone reported against that population — my 7-of-15, a sibling's 1-of-61
— was poor, and everyone including me blamed the similarity matcher.

> **A DIFFERENT-STATE FILTER SELECTS AGAINST TRUE DUPLICATES, because two slices
> stating one capability usually AGREE about it.**

The whole effort was mining the class where a true duplicate is **least likely
to be, by construction**. Corrected, the contrast is **13 of 16 at the head
against 7 of 15** — same matcher, same corpus, opposite population.

**Every precision number published for that instrument was measuring the filter.**

## 3. WHY VERDICTS DO NOT PROPAGATE — one ruling, three names

Of 133 GAP-vs-EXCLUDED-RULED pairs, **32 cite a named ruling and all 32 are in
`network.md`**; the other 101 cite nothing. `profile.md` alone held **86 verdicts
with no citable basis** (87% of its ruled rows).

And underneath that, the mechanism:

> **One shipped sentence — `server.py::linkedin_update_setting`, *"a setting is
> admitted by name or not at all"* — appears in the census under THREE names:
> `R11`, `MESSAGING-SETTINGS (3.10)`, and the bare prose `settings family`.**
>
> **A ruling that is RE-DERIVED rather than CITED cannot propagate, because
> there is nothing to search for.**

The case that proves it is recursive: `M M42`'s note says it was re-filed
because *"two census slices applied it differently"* — **and that wave left its
own twin `N 170` GAP for two weeks.** The disease recurred inside its own cure,
because the fix minted the third name instead of citing the first.

**I walked past it too** — I quoted that sentence the same morning, having read
`R11` in the same session. Four encounters before anyone joined them. **32 rows
now carry the symbol plus both existing ids; no fourth name was minted.**

## 4. THE DISCRIMINATOR THAT MADE THE FLIPS SAFE

Two rows can sit GAP beside an EXCLUDED-RULED twin and mean opposite things:

| row | its note says | reading |
|---|---|---|
| `M C69` | *"reaches third parties; `/invite` is a forbidden substring"* | **states the ruling's own premise and files GAP anyway** — propagation failure, flip |
| `P E6` | *"the written reason does not reach this act"* | **argues the ruling's scope excludes it** — considered exception, leave |

> **The test is not whether a ruling is nearby. It is whether anybody weighed
> its SCOPE.**

**No mechanical test separates those**, which is also why the zero-guard is not
buildable (§6).

| 1 | blocker-20 row identification | *"exact on two independent axes"* — 11 rows, 11W | **one axis wearing two names.** Two row sets matched; the test identified nothing. The property that decides membership — *was the row GAP at the frozen commit* — I never checked |

**Five and six are the same class**, and it is the class I had diagnosed against
the census twice that morning: *a dialect the instrument does not speak.*
**`count_census_states.py` documents that exact defect against itself** — `XR`
cost it 23 invisible rows — **and I read that docstring the same morning and
wrote the same defect anyway, twice.**

> **Prose does not prevent. Not even prose you have just read, in the file you
> are using, about the defect you are about to write.**

## 6. THE ARC THAT PAID FOR THE DISCIPLINE

I published **Amendment H**: *"the published 761 does not reconcile, and one of
its two terms is missing."* It was wrong — failure #6.

**But it was filed as *"I cannot reproduce this derivation"*, not as *"the
denominator is inflated by 44"***, and it named three futures that fit the
evidence, noting that two of them move the number in opposite directions.

> **Had it asserted the 44, this would have been retracting a CLAIM rather than
> a SEARCH** — and the 44 would have propagated into every ratio in the
> campaign.

It cost an hour. `761` reconciles to within one row; both collapsed blocks
exist; **nothing needed changing.** H is retracted in place and left standing so
the correction has a referent — the same method used on the blocker-20 headline.

**That is the whole payoff of refusing to round a search up into a claim.**

## 7. WHAT WAS DELIBERATELY NOT BUILT

* **No zero-guard.** A cell is not a read — **79%** carry no date, **37** span
  two or more. And the best narrow design **fires on `network.md` 174**, the one
  row that says *"A ZERO CANNOT SETTLE THIS ROW"*. **A check that convicts the
  most careful row in the corpus would train waves to write vaguer notes.**
* **No notation minted.** Even a perfect one does not fix row 174, and minting
  a fourth name is §3's disease at the schema level. `**MEASURED <date>` is
  spreading on its own — six instances — which is the only way a notation
  survives.
* **No tail estimate.** 229 swept, **13 read, 216 not.** `229 × 0.81` would be a
  fabrication with a decimal point.
* **No subtraction without a conditional.** The four executed had one written in
  `messaging` s10 and I measured its trigger met. The thirteen do not; they are
  registered, not applied.

## 8. THE LAWS, IN THE FORM THEY SURVIVED IN

> **A zero taken alongside a positive from the same read is SELF-CONTROLLING.**
> The positive proves the surface rendered, so the zero is about the data.
> *(A review question, not a guard — it paid for itself when a shell report was
> raised against my profile rows and every one survived.)*

> **Two readers agreeing is not evidence when both made the same unchecked
> assumption — it is one error with two witnesses.**
> *(And its positive form, four hours later: two waves agreeing after measuring
> DIFFERENT things is worth far more than two agreeing after measuring the
> same thing.)*

> **The score generates candidates. It must never settle one.**
> *(Every true finding this round came from an artifact — a ruling id, a code
> predicate, a neighbour that agrees. None came from a number I chose, and all
> six failures above were numbers I chose.)*

> **An impossible value in your own output is a better lead than a plausible
> one.** *(`jobs 17` showed the state `R`, which is not a state. Chasing it found
> the ranges table carrying R/W for 84 rows, and 13 ambiguous row ids.)*

## 9. WHAT IS LEFT, HONESTLY

* **216 same-state candidates unread.** Head precision was 13 of 16; the tail is
  unmeasured and I decline to estimate it.
* **13 duplicates registered, not subtracted** — no conditional exists for
  `jobs`↔`profile` or `profile`↔`network`, so each subtraction is a new
  decision and none has been asked for. **10 clean; 2 bundled, which are not
  subtractable at all; and 1 containment, now RULED.**
* **The containment is settled as a rule and open only as a reading**
  (`cdcf14d` s7): **the grain follows what the platform DRAWS.** One control
  with a reason parameter is one capability; two controls are two. *A
  parameter value is not a capability.* Both rows stay until somebody reads
  which shape that control has — **one read of an already-admitted surface.**
  **That is a ruling with a missing input, not a deferral**, and the next wave
  measures rather than re-argues.
* **4 more double-counts found in a collapsed block** (`N67`/`N68`/`N71`/`N74`
  against `P O6-O20`), answering a question open in `network.md` since before
  today — also unexecuted, and the note states its own authority as *"a
  top-level de-duplication job, not a slice one."*
* **The `jobs.md` R/W column**: approved in principle, deferred in execution,
  **empty beats guessed**. 84 of 151 rows already have a value in that file's own
  ranges table — found only because an impossible value led there.

## 10. TWO OPERATIONAL HAZARDS FROM THE LAST HOUR, both cheap and both real

**1. `add && verify && commit` EARNED ITS PLACE, on its first use.** The verify
step read **three** files in the index where one was expected — a sibling had
staged two between my `git add` and the check. **The `&&` chain stopped and the
commit never ran.** Without it, two files I had never read would have ridden
into a commit of mine. `--only` then committed exactly my path.

**2. A COMMIT MESSAGE IN `/tmp` IS NOT DURABLE HERE, and this is new.** The
standing rule is *message as a FILE PATH, never stdin* — it does not say where
the file goes. In git-bash on this box `/tmp` maps to the Windows user temp
directory, which **is cleaned underneath a running session**: a message written
minutes earlier was gone at commit time —

    fatal: could not read log file '/tmp/msg-....txt': No such file or directory

and because the message vanished rather than the content being wrong, **the
failure looks like a git problem rather than a storage one.**

> **Write commit messages into the session scratchpad, not `/tmp`.** The rule
> that saves you from stdin does not save you from a temp sweeper, and the two
> failures are indistinguishable from the error text.
