## 9. THE READER, AND THE CONTROL THAT MADE ITS ZERO MEAN ANYTHING

`linkedin_server/collections_page.py` (`7d99575`), with

### 9.1 The vocabulary goes INTO the page, and an integer comes back

Section 8 established that `read_surface_census` cannot serve as a parser
because it shapes by construction. The reader answers that by reversing the
direction of travel:

> The five groupings are shipped into the page, the comparison happens in the
> document, and **what crosses the CDP boundary is a POSITION IN A TUPLE**
> defined in the module.

A label is therefore not redacted, not shaped, and **not present in this
process at all**. That is a stronger claim than either sibling makes --
`groups.py` takes hrefs somebody else read, and `menus.py`'s `classify` is a
pure function that IS handed a label and declines to return any of it. Neither
needs an argument about what a shaper can recognise; this needs none either,
for a simpler reason.

**The vocabulary is LinkedIn's**, from the census row sourced to help article
`a1652837` -- not read off the page. Inventing a closed set from what a page
happens to draw is how it stops being closed.

**No label is a parameter of any publishing function**, asserted on
`inspect.signature`. The reader's one string parameter is the control path and
is asserted as such, because the whole safety argument depends on it not being
a second route for page text.
