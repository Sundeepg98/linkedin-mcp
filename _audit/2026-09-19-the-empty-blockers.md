## 1. THE COLUMN LABELLED "survive the split" IS A SUBSET COUNT, NOT A ROW COUNT

This is the most reusable finding of the wave, and it dissolves the anomaly the
first version of this file raised.

Section 47 of `_audit/_scratch/_progress-unlocatable-recovery.md` publishes
`PER-MESSAGE-OVERFLOW-MENU` as **"8 candidates for 2 slots; 21 survive the
split"**. (That path is GITIGNORED working notes and reaches no clone, so every
cell this document uses from it is QUOTED here in full rather than deferred to.
The quotations are the evidence; the path is provenance.) A subset cannot
outnumber its superset, so the cell cannot be read as written -- that much the
twelve-minute version got right. Its DIAGNOSIS was
wrong. I wrote that one of the two counts was computed over a different set, and
concluded the blocker "has no verified axis diagnosis at all". Neither number is
wrong. **The LABEL is wrong**, and the quantity is a different KIND.

**The proof, on two independent grounds.**

*Ground one, the arithmetic identity.* If the column counts candidate SUBSETS
that satisfy the published split rather than candidate ROWS that survive a
filter, then for a `2W` blocker with `w` write-candidates the value is
`C(w, 2)`. Here `C(7, 2) = 21` exactly, and `7 <= 8` candidates. Every other row
of section 47 is consistent with the same rule, which is why nobody caught it --
for a `1W` blocker the value is `C(w, 1) = w`, which never exceeds the candidate
count and so always looks like a survivor count:

| blocker | split | cands | column | as a subset count |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| `MESSAGE-REACTION` | 1W | 8 | 7 | `C(7,1)` -- 7 of 8 are W |
| `MESSAGE-ADDRESSING` | 1W | 8 | 7 | `C(7,1)` |
| `INVITE-NOTE-PARAM` | 1W | 4 | 4 | `C(4,1)` -- all W |
| `INMAIL-COMPOSE-SURFACE` | 1W | 2 | 2 | `C(2,1)` |
| `FOUND-A-JOB-FLOW` | 1W | 5 | 2 | `C(2,1)` -- 2 of 5 are W |
| `EASY-APPLY-MULTISTEP` | 1W | 3 | 3 | `C(3,1)` |
| `FEED-CONTENT-READ-RULING` | 2R | 5 | 3 | `C(3,2)` -- 3 of 5 are R |
| `PER-MESSAGE-OVERFLOW-MENU` | 2W | 8 | **21** | `C(7,2)` -- 7 of 8 are W |
| `THREAD-REPLY-BOX` | 2W | 1 | 0 | `C(1,2) = 0` |
| `POST-COMMENT-CONTROLS` | 1R/3W | 3 | 0 | 4 rows cannot come from 3 |
| `CONVERSATION-OVERFLOW-MENU` | 1R/8W/1RW | 6 | 0 | 10 rows cannot come from 6 |

**`2W` is the only shape in the whole table where a subset count can exceed its
candidate count**, and `PER-MESSAGE-OVERFLOW-MENU` is the only `2W` blocker with
more than one candidate. The anomaly had exactly one place it could surface, and
it surfaced there.

*Ground two, the recovery's own vocabulary.* Section 38, written earlier the same
day, reports the identical quantity in words: *"`EASY-APPLY-MULTISTEP` went to 3
subsets, `INMAIL-COMPOSE-SURFACE` to 2."* Section 47's column for those two
blockers reads **3** and **2**. Same instrument, same numbers, and the earlier
prose calls them SUBSETS. Section 37 does it again -- *"de-blinding the matcher
took it from 'unique' to 45 candidate subsets."* The word was right in the prose
and got lost in the table header.

**THE ROUTING CONSEQUENCE, WHICH REVERSES MY OWN EARLIER CLAIM.** I said the
broken cell meant nobody could tell whether this blocker faced a narrow contest
(8) or a wide one (21), and that it therefore had no verified axis. That was
wrong in the direction that manufactures work. There was never a wide search: the
candidate count is 8, the 21 is the number of ways to choose 2 of the 7 writes
among them, and **section 47's routing verdict -- "needs a RULING" -- was correct
all along.** The defect is a mislabelled column, and it is a documentation fix,
not a recount. The fix is to rename the column to `subsets` wherever that
instrument prints.

This is the "check whether you are looking for the wrong KIND" tool turned on my
own finding. I had the right anomaly and the wrong kind.

**A REFINEMENT OF MY OWN EARLIER FINDING 2, because it was stated too broadly.**
I wrote that under-supplied blockers "are searches, not contests" and that
counting cannot reach them. The first half stands; the second was imprecise.
Counting cannot CREATE candidates, so it cannot fill the missing slot -- but a
SEARCH can supply candidates, and once supplied the count can still force the
nominated ones. `JOBCARD-OVERFLOW-MENU` went from a published zero candidates to
COMPLETE on exactly that route. **Under-supply blocks the arithmetic, not the
evidence.** The correct advice to a successor is: do not point COUNTING at an
under-supplied blocker; do point the REASON CELL at it.

## 5. THE FALSE-POSITIVE CLASS WORTH REGISTERING

A corpus sweep for "a row id within N lines of a blocker name" fires on the
LEDGER INDEX, because both are small integers and the probe files write the index
directly above the blocker name (`# 65 JOBCARD-OVERFLOW-MENU`,
`# 81 FOUND-A-JOB-FLOW`, `ROW 57 MESSAGE-ADDRESSING`). It flagged all seven
blockers swept; **two of those flags were the index, not a row.**

The discriminator is cheap and should be built into any future sweep: a census
row id is SLICE-QUALIFIED (`J 28`, `M M48`, `M C43`), a ledger index is a bare
integer. A sweep that does not require the slice letter will keep reporting
blockers as located when nothing has been located.
